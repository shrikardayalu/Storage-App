You recorded something worth sharing, hit send, and got told it's too large. That's normal for long iPhone videos: Apple's own guide to [sharing long videos](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/share-long-videos-iph1c81f302d/ios) notes that some are too big for a standard text message. The fix depends on where the video needs to go.

## Why does iPhone say the video is too large?

A text message or a regular email has a size cap, and Apple says the limit for attachments is set by your service provider, not by the iPhone. A few minutes of high-resolution video can easily go past it. Your phone isn't broken, and the video isn't damaged. You just need a route that doesn't squeeze the file through a small pipe.

There are four:

| Route | Best when | Size limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AirDrop | The other person is nearby with an Apple device | None stated by Apple |
| Mail Drop | They're far away, or don't use Apple devices | 5 GB per message |
| Trim | Only part of the video matters | Whatever's left |
| Smaller copy | It has to go through a text or a messaging app | Whatever the copy comes to |

## Send it with AirDrop (nearby Apple devices)

AirDrop sends the full video straight to a nearby iPhone, iPad, Mac, or Apple Vision Pro. Apple says the devices need to be within 30 feet (10 meters) of each other, with **Wi-Fi** and **Bluetooth** turned on for both of you.

1. If the other person isn't in your contacts, ask them to set AirDrop to **Everyone for 10 Minutes** in **Settings → General → AirDrop**.
2. Open **Photos** and tap the video.
3. Tap the **Share** button.
4. Optional: tap **Options** and turn on **All Photos Data** to send the original format with its location, edit history, and captions.
5. Tap **AirDrop**, then tap their name or device.
6. They get an alert and tap **Accept**.

If you walk out of range after the transfer starts, Apple says it carries on over the internet. That can use cellular data, which matters for a big file.

## Send it by email with Mail Drop (anyone, anywhere)

Mail Drop is the Mail app's answer to large attachments. Instead of attaching the video to the email, it uploads it to iCloud and sends the recipient a link. According to Apple's [Mail Drop limits](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108329):

- Attachments can be up to **5 GB**.
- The recipient has **30 days** to download them.
- Mail Drop attachments **don't count against your iCloud storage**.

To send a video this way:

1. Open **Photos** and tap the video.
2. Tap **Share**, then tap **Mail**.
3. Enter the recipient in **To**, and add a subject or message if you like.
4. Tap **Send**. If the video is over your email provider's limit, Mail asks whether to use Mail Drop. Follow the prompt.

Two things can still get in the way. A message over 5 GB can't go through Mail Drop at all, so you'd need to send a shorter or smaller version. And the recipient's email app may have a lower message size limit of its own. Uploading a large video also takes time on a slow connection, so start it on Wi-Fi if you can.

## Trim it to the part that matters

Often the person only needs 30 seconds of a five-minute clip. Trimming fixes the size problem and makes the video better to watch.

1. Open **Photos** and tap the video.
2. Tap **Edit**.
3. Drag either end of the frame viewer under the video to set where it starts and stops.
4. Tap **Done**.
5. Tap **Save Video as New Clip** to keep the full original as well, or **Save Video** to keep only the trimmed version.

Then share the trimmed clip as usual. Which save option to pick if you also want the space back is in [the trimming guide](/guides/trim-video-iphone). If it's still too large for a text, try AirDrop or Mail Drop with it, or make it smaller.

## Make a smaller copy

When it has to go through a text or a messaging app, the video needs to be smaller, not just shorter. Lowering the resolution is what does that. iPhone has no compress button in Photos, but the built-in **Shortcuts** app can re-encode a video at a lower resolution. Here's [how to compress videos on iPhone](/guides/compress-videos-iphone), with the Shortcuts steps and what each setting does to quality.

A smaller copy is usually fine on another phone's screen. Keep the original if you might want it for a TV or for editing later.

## Stop it happening next time

Big videos are hard to send and hard to keep. Two settings help with both:

- **Settings → Camera → Record Video:** this screen shows roughly how much space a minute of video takes at each setting. Picking a lower resolution for everyday clips makes every video smaller from the start.
- **Settings → Camera → Formats:** **High Efficiency** records smaller files than **Most Compatible**. The [High Efficiency vs Most Compatible guide](/guides/high-efficiency-vs-most-compatible-iphone) explains the trade-off, including how your iPhone shares copies that open on other devices.

If your videos are too big to send, they're probably also why your phone is short on space. Here's [what to clear first when iPhone storage is full](/guides/iphone-storage-full).
