When your iPhone says storage is full, the temptation is to start deleting at random. Don't. On almost every iPhone, a small number of large files are responsible for most of the problem. Find those first and you'll often clear several gigabytes without touching anything you care about.

## Step 1: See what's actually using the space

Go to **Settings → General → iPhone Storage**. Give it a few seconds to calculate. You'll see:

- A colored bar showing how your storage splits between apps, photos, media, iOS, and System Data.
- **Recommendations**, such as offloading unused apps or reviewing large attachments.
- A list of apps sorted by size, from largest to smallest.

Tap any app to see how much is the app itself (**App Size**) versus what it has stored (**Documents & Data**). That split tells you whether deleting the app would help or whether it's the content inside it.

## Step 2: Start with the biggest files: videos

For most people, **Photos** sits at or near the top of that list, and within Photos, videos do most of the damage. According to the estimates in **Settings → Camera → Record Video**, one minute of 4K video at 60 fps takes roughly 400 MB, compared with about 60 MB for 1080p at 30 fps. Ten minutes of 4K footage you'll never rewatch can outweigh a thousand photos.

To find them in the Photos app:

1. Open **Photos** and scroll down to **Media Types**.
2. Tap **Videos**. You'll see every video in your library.
3. Delete the ones you don't need: duplicates of the same moment, accidental recordings, long clips you only needed a few seconds of.

The Photos app doesn't sort videos by file size, so this step involves some guessing. Long videos and anything shot in 4K or slo-mo are the usual suspects.

## Step 3: Shrink what you want to keep

Deleting isn't the only way to get space back, and for photos and videos you care about it's the worst one. Three ways to make files smaller instead:

- **Compress or re-encode videos.** A 4K video re-saved at 1080p is a fraction of the size and looks nearly the same on a phone. See [how to compress videos on iPhone](/guides/compress-videos-iphone).
- **Trim long videos.** Keep the 30 seconds that matter and drop the rest. Trimming covered in the same [video guide](/guides/compress-videos-iphone).
- **Keep Live Photos as stills.** Every Live Photo stores a short video you rarely watch. Here's [how to keep them as stills](/guides/live-photos-storage-iphone) so the space is actually freed.

## Step 4: Clear screenshots and duplicates

These are small individually but add up over years.

- **Screenshots:** In Photos, go to **Media Types → Screenshots**. Most people have hundreds they no longer need. See [how to delete all your screenshots at once](/guides/delete-all-screenshots-iphone).
- **Duplicates:** In Photos, go to **Utilities → Duplicates** (iOS 16 and later). Tap **Merge** to keep the best copy of each set. Full walkthrough: [find and delete duplicate photos](/guides/delete-duplicate-photos-iphone).

## Step 5: Empty Recently Deleted

This is the step everyone forgets. Deleted photos and videos go to **Recently Deleted**, where they stay for about 30 days and **keep using storage** until then.

1. In Photos, scroll to **Utilities → Recently Deleted**.
2. Unlock it with Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode.
3. Tap **Select**, then **Delete All**.

Only do this once you're sure. After this, the items are gone.

## Step 6: Let iOS handle the rest

A few built-in settings keep storage from filling back up:

| Setting | Where to find it | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offload Unused Apps | Settings → General → iPhone Storage (in Recommendations) | Removes apps you don't use but keeps their data |
| Optimize iPhone Storage | Settings → Photos (requires iCloud Photos) | Keeps full-size originals in iCloud and smaller versions on your phone |
| Keep Messages | Settings → Messages → Keep Messages | Automatically deletes messages older than 30 days or 1 year |
| Record Video format | Settings → Camera → Record Video | Lower resolution or frame rate means smaller new videos |

## What not to delete

- **Apps with data you can't recreate**, such as notes apps or games with local saves, unless you've checked they sync to the cloud.
- **Your only copy of a photo.** If you don't use iCloud Photos or another backup, back up your library before a big cleanup.
- **Anything a "cleaner" app tells you is junk** without showing you exactly what it is. Read [what iPhone cleaner apps can and can't do](/guides/are-iphone-cleaner-apps-safe) first.

## The short version

Check **iPhone Storage**, go after videos first, and shrink before you delete: compress, trim, and keep Live Photos as stills. Then clear true clutter and empty **Recently Deleted**. For most people, that gets back more space than everything else combined.
