You held the shutter a little too long and now one moment is a stack of near-identical frames. A burst is great for catching the one shot where nobody blinked, but every frame you keep is a full photo on your phone.

## What a burst photo is

A burst is a run of photos taken in rapid succession while you hold the shutter. In your library it shows up as a single thumbnail, which makes it easy to forget how many photos are behind it.

To take one, open **Camera** and drag the **Shutter** button to the left. Lift your finger to stop. You can also turn on **Settings → Camera → Use Volume Up for Burst** and hold the volume up button instead ([Apple's Burst mode guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/take-burst-mode-shots-ipha42c55cd0/ios)).

## How to keep only the best shot from a burst

1. Open **Photos** and tap the burst.
2. Tap **Burst (number)** at the top left of the screen.
3. Swipe through the frames. Gray dots below the thumbnails mark the ones your iPhone suggests keeping.
4. Tap the circle in the lower-right corner of each frame you want to save as its own photo.
5. Tap **Done**.

The frames you pick are saved as individual photos. If iOS then asks whether to keep the rest of the burst, choose the option that keeps only your picks if you want the space back.

## How to delete an entire burst

If none of the frames are worth keeping:

1. Open the burst in **Photos** (or tap the thumbnail in **Camera** right after taking it).
2. Tap the **Delete** button.

That removes every frame in the burst at once.

## Get the space back: empty Recently Deleted

Deleted photos don't leave your phone right away. They go to **Recently Deleted** for 30 days, and you can recover them or delete them permanently at any time in that window ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/delete-or-hide-photos-and-videos-iphb4defbde9/ios)). Until then, they still take up space.

If you've just cleared a few bursts and want the storage now, [empty Recently Deleted on your iPhone](/guides/empty-recently-deleted-iphone) once you're sure.

If you use iCloud Photos, deletes sync: a burst you remove on your iPhone is removed from iCloud and your other devices too. Keep a copy elsewhere first if you're unsure.

## Doing this for dozens of bursts

The built-in route is fine for a handful. It gets slow when you have years of bursts, because each one is opened, sorted, and confirmed separately.

## Stop bursts from piling up

- Tap the shutter instead of holding or dragging it when you only want one photo.
- If you turned on **Use Volume Up for Burst** and keep triggering it by accident, turn it off in **Settings → Camera**.
- Go through new bursts the same day, while you still remember which frame was the good one.
