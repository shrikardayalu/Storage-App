You filmed the whole thing and only the middle went wrong: a stranger walked through, someone dropped the phone, or there are five minutes of nothing between the two moments you care about. The obvious fix, the trim tool in Photos, can't help, because it only works from the ends.

## Why Photos can't cut the middle

Apple's steps for [trimming a video on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/trim-video-length-adjust-speed-and-edit-audio-iph1d1344a2d/ios) are all about the two ends: you drag either end of the frame viewer to change where the video starts and stops, then tap **Done**. There's no way to pick a section in the middle and remove it.

So you have two routes: iMovie, which can actually remove the section and give you one video, or a Photos-only workaround that leaves you with two clips.

## How to cut out the middle with iMovie

iMovie is Apple's own video editor. It's listed as free on the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/imovie/id377298193). These steps come from Apple's [iMovie guide for iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/imovie-iphone/knac788312/ios).

**Start a project with your video**

1. Open **iMovie** and tap **Start New Project**, then **Movie**. (If you don't see it, tap **Projects** first.)
2. Tap the video you want to fix, then tap **Create Movie** at the bottom of the screen.

**Split out the part you don't want**

3. Scroll the timeline until the playhead, the white vertical line, sits where the unwanted part begins. Pinch open on the timeline to zoom in if you need to be precise.
4. Tap the clip, tap the **Actions** button, then tap **Split**. (Or tap the clip and swipe down over the playhead.) Make sure the playhead isn't at the very start of the clip, or there's nothing to split.
5. Scroll to where the unwanted part ends and split again the same way.
6. Tap the piece in the middle, then tap **Delete**. If you delete the wrong piece, tap **Undo** at the top of the screen.

Apple says iMovie puts a plain cut between the two clips you split, so the finished video jumps straight from the part before to the part after. Play it back from the start to check the joins.

**Save it to your library**

7. Go back to the **Projects** browser and tap your project.
8. Tap the **Share** button, then **Save or Share Video**.
9. Tap **Options** if you want to choose the resolution or frame rate, then tap **Done**.
10. Tap **Save Video**.

To remove more than one section, repeat steps 3 to 6 for each one before you save.

## The Photos-only workaround

If you don't want another app, you can get close with Photos by keeping the good parts as two separate videos:

1. Open the video in **Photos** and tap **Edit**.
2. Drag the end of the frame viewer back to where the unwanted part begins, tap **Done**, then **Save Video as New Clip**. That's your "before" clip.
3. Open the full-length original again, tap **Edit**, and drag the start of the frame viewer to where the unwanted part ends. Tap **Done**, then **Save Video as New Clip**. That's your "after" clip.

Apple says **Save Video as New Clip** keeps both versions, so the original is untouched until you delete it. You end up with two videos instead of one, which is fine for keeping memories and awkward for sending. Our [guide to trimming videos on iPhone](/guides/trim-video-iphone) explains the difference between the two save buttons in more detail.

## Don't end up with two copies

Both routes leave you with a new video and, most likely, the full-length original still sitting next to it in Photos. For a long recording that can double the space it takes up.

Once you've played the new version and you're happy with it:

1. Open the full-length original in Photos and delete it.
2. Empty **Recently Deleted**. Apple says deleted videos stay there for 30 days, and they still take up space until then. Here's [how to empty Recently Deleted on iPhone](/guides/empty-recently-deleted-iphone).

If you use iCloud Photos, Apple says deleting a video removes it from all your devices, so check the edit first.

Two more things worth knowing. iMovie itself is about 700 MB, according to its App Store page, so if you only needed it once, delete the app afterwards. And if the edited video is still too big, the size comes from how it was recorded rather than how long it runs. Here's [how to compress videos on iPhone](/guides/compress-videos-iphone) to make a smaller copy.

## When cutting isn't the real problem

Most people reach for a video editor because one clip is in the way. If you're doing it because your phone is full, one edited video rarely changes much. The bigger win is dealing with all the long recordings at once. If you're not sure videos are what's filling the phone, start with [what to clear first when iPhone storage is full](/guides/iphone-storage-full).
