You deleted a pile of photos, checked your storage, and the number barely moved. That's not a glitch. On iPhone, deleting something usually just moves it to a **Recently Deleted** folder, where it waits, still taking up space, so you can change your mind.

There isn't one trash can for the whole phone. Photos, Files, and Messages each keep their own, and each one has to be emptied separately.

## Why deleted photos still use storage

Apple keeps deleted photos and videos in the **Recently Deleted** collection for 30 days before removing them permanently. During those 30 days you can recover them or delete them for good whenever you like. Until one of those happens, they still count against your storage.

That's why emptying Recently Deleted is the last step of any cleanup, whether you [cleared duplicate photos](/guides/delete-duplicate-photos-iphone), deleted screenshots, or replaced big videos with smaller copies.

## How to empty Recently Deleted in Photos

1. Open **Photos**.
2. Scroll down and tap **Recently Deleted** (below **Utilities**).
3. Tap **View Album**, then unlock with **Face ID**, **Touch ID**, or your **passcode**.
4. Tap **Select**, then choose the photos and videos you want gone.
5. Tap the **More** button (the three dots), then tap **Delete**.

If you want to clear the whole album, some iOS versions also show a **Delete All** option after you tap **Select**. Either way, there's no undo once it's done.

**Before you tap Delete, check two things:**

- **Is anything in there you still want?** Scroll through first. If you find something, select it and choose **Recover** from the same **More** menu, and it goes back to your library.
- **Do you use iCloud Photos?** If so, anything you delete is deleted on all your devices. Permanently deleting a photo removes it from all your devices, so if iCloud is your only other copy, it's gone everywhere. Apple explains this in [Delete or hide photos and videos on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/delete-or-hide-photos-and-videos-iphb4defbde9/ios).

Once it's empty, open **Settings → General → iPhone Storage** to check the result. The **Photos** figure in the app list is the one to watch.

## When emptying it isn't enough

Recently Deleted only holds what you've already deleted. If your storage is still tight after emptying it, the space is in things you've kept, and for most people that means videos.

You don't have to delete those to get the space back. A long 4K video can be [compressed to a smaller copy](/guides/compress-videos-iphone) or trimmed to the part that matters, and Live Photos can be [kept as plain stills](/guides/live-photos-storage-iphone). When you then delete the originals, they wait in Recently Deleted like anything else, so you can compare before you empty it.

## How to empty Recently Deleted in Files

The Files app keeps its own Recently Deleted for files you remove from **iCloud Drive** or **On My iPhone**: downloads, PDFs, saved videos, and anything else you've stored there.

1. Open **Files** and tap the **Browse** tab.
2. Under **Locations**, tap **Recently Deleted**.
3. Tap the **More** button, then tap **Select**.
4. Choose the files, then tap the **Delete** button.

Apple says files deleted from Recently Deleted are deleted immediately and can't be recovered. Anything you leave there is permanently deleted after 30 days. If you change your mind before then, select it and tap **Recover**, and it returns to its original location ([Apple's Files instructions](https://support.apple.com/104953)).

## How to empty Recently Deleted in Messages

Since iOS 16, deleted texts also go to a Recently Deleted folder instead of disappearing, and Apple says deleted attachments stay there too, for up to 30 days.

1. Open **Messages**.
2. On the conversation list, tap the **Filter** button. (On iOS 18, tap **Edit** instead.)
3. Tap **Recently Deleted**.
4. Select the conversations whose messages you want to delete permanently.
5. Tap the **Delete** button, then tap **Delete** again to confirm.

Apple notes that this can't be undone. If you use Messages in iCloud, deleting a message or conversation on your iPhone also deletes it from your other devices where Messages in iCloud is turned on.

## Which Recently Deleted should you empty first?

| App | Where to find it | How long items stay | Can you undo after emptying? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Photos | Photos → Recently Deleted (below Utilities) | 30 days | No |
| Files | Files → Browse → Locations → Recently Deleted | 30 days | No |
| Messages | Messages → Filter (Edit on iOS 18) → Recently Deleted | Up to 30 days (Apple's recovery article says 30 to 40) | No |

For most people, Photos is the one that matters. It's where the large files are. To see which apps use the most space on your phone, open **Settings → General → iPhone Storage**: it lists apps by size and may offer recommendations, which you can review before anything is removed.

## Keep it from filling up again

Recently Deleted empties itself after about 30 days, so you only need to step in when you want the space back now. The bigger win is making fewer, smaller files in the first place: shrink videos instead of keeping huge originals, and set **Settings → Apps → Messages → Keep Messages** to a shorter period if old conversations pile up.

For the full order of what to clear first, see [what to delete when your iPhone storage is full](/guides/iphone-storage-full).
