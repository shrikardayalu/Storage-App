You open **Settings → General → iPhone Storage**, wait for the bar to load, and there it is: a gray chunk labeled **System Data** taking up 15, 20, sometimes 40 GB. It's not photos, not apps, and there's no delete button. Here's what's going on.

## What System Data actually is

System Data is a catch-all for files iOS creates and manages for itself. Apple's description of it is deliberately broad, but in practice it includes:

- **Caches** from streaming music, video, and podcasts
- **Temporary files** apps create while they work
- **Logs and diagnostics**
- **Downloaded resources** such as Siri voices, fonts, and dictionaries
- **Leftovers from iOS updates**

Most of these are designed to be temporary. iOS clears caches automatically when it needs space, which is why System Data sometimes shrinks on its own when your iPhone gets close to full.

## Why it gets so big

The usual causes:

- **Heavy streaming.** Apps that stream video and music cache content locally.
- **Messages with lots of media.** Attachments and their previews add up.
- **Safari's website data.** Years of browsing leaves a lot behind.
- **A recent iOS update.** Update files sometimes linger for a while after installing.

## Fixes that actually work

Try these in order. Check **iPhone Storage** after each one; the number can take a few minutes to update.

### 1. Restart your iPhone

It sounds too simple, but restarting clears some temporary files and often drops System Data by a few gigabytes. Press and hold the side button and either volume button, then slide to power off.

### 2. Clear Safari's history and website data

Go to **Settings → Apps → Safari → Clear History and Website Data** (on older iOS versions, **Settings → Safari**). This signs you out of websites, so make sure you know your passwords or use a password manager.

### 3. Keep messages for less time

Go to **Settings → Apps → Messages → Keep Messages** and choose **1 Year** or **30 Days**. iOS will offer to delete older messages and their attachments. Before you do, open **Settings → General → iPhone Storage → Messages** and use **Review Large Attachments** to save anything you want to keep.

### 4. Delete and reinstall apps with big caches

In **iPhone Storage**, tap an app and look at **Documents & Data**. If it's in the gigabytes for a streaming or social app, deleting and reinstalling it usually clears that cache. Make sure the app syncs your data to an account before you delete it.

### 5. Update iOS

Updates often include storage management fixes. Go to **Settings → General → Software Update**.

### 6. Last resort: back up and restore

If System Data stays huge for weeks, a full backup and restore rebuilds it from scratch. It takes time and carries some risk, so only do it with a complete, verified backup.

## What doesn't work

- **"Junk cleaner" apps.** iOS sandboxes every app. No App Store app can see or delete other apps' caches or system files, no matter what its screenshots show.
- **Waiting for a magic setting.** There's no toggle that clears System Data on demand.

## Focus on the space you control

System Data is usually not the biggest thing on your iPhone. For most people, **photos and videos** are. If you're trying to free up space, that's where the gigabytes are. Start with our guide to [what to delete when iPhone storage is full](/guides/iphone-storage-full).
