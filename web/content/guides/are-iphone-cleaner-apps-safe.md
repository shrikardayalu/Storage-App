Search the App Store for "cleaner" and you'll find dozens of apps promising to speed up your iPhone, remove junk, and protect you from threats. Some are useful. Many overpromise. The difference comes down to what iOS actually allows apps to do.

## What iPhone cleaner apps can actually access

Every iPhone app runs in a **sandbox**: it can see its own files and nothing else, unless you grant permission to specific data. For a cleaner app, that means:

| Can access (with your permission) | Can't access, ever |
| --- | --- |
| Photos and videos | Other apps' files or caches |
| Contacts | System Data or iOS system files |
| Calendar events | Memory (RAM) or running processes |
| Its own storage | Your messages or email |

So a legitimate cleaner app can help you find **large videos, screenshots, duplicate photos, and duplicate contacts**. That's genuinely useful, because those are where most of your space goes. But that's the ceiling.

## Claims that should make you suspicious

- **"Boost RAM" or "speed up your phone."** iOS manages memory itself. Apps can't free another app's memory.
- **"Virus detected!"** App Store apps can't scan the system. Fake threat warnings are a scare tactic.
- **"Clean junk files" or "clear System Data."** Not possible. See [why System Data is so large](/guides/iphone-system-data-too-large) for what actually helps.
- **A "scan" that finds problems before you've granted any permission.** It hasn't scanned anything.

## Red flags in pricing

Many cleaner apps make their money from subscriptions. Be careful when you see:

- A **paywall before you can see any results**.
- **Weekly subscriptions** with a short free trial that's easy to forget.
- A close button that's tiny, delayed, or hidden.

You can review and cancel subscriptions anytime in **Settings → [your name] → Subscriptions**.

## How to check an app before installing

1. **Read the privacy label.** On the App Store listing, scroll to **App Privacy** to see what data the app collects and whether it's linked to you.
2. **Read recent one-star reviews.** They're where billing surprises show up.
3. **Check what it asks for.** A photo cleaner needs photo access. It doesn't need your location or contacts.
4. **Prefer apps that show you exactly what they'll delete** and leave the final decision to you.

## The safe default

You can do everything a cleaner app does with built-in tools. It just takes longer. Our guide on [what to delete when your iPhone storage is full](/guides/iphone-storage-full) walks through it. A good cleaner app just makes those same steps faster.
