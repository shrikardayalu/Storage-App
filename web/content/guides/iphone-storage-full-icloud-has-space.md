Your iPhone says it's out of space, yet iCloud says you have gigabytes free. It looks like a contradiction, and it comes up again and again in Apple's community forums. The short answer: the two aren't connected the way most people assume.

## Why your iPhone is full when iCloud isn't

Apple describes them as two different things in [What's the difference between iCloud storage and device storage](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102670):

- **Device storage** is the storage built into your iPhone. It holds your apps, photos, music, and everything else that's on the phone.
- **iCloud storage** is space in the cloud, tied to your Apple Account. It holds iCloud Backups, iCloud Photos, iCloud Drive, and data that syncs between your devices. Everyone gets 5 GB free; more comes with an iCloud+ plan.

Free space in one doesn't spill over into the other. A 2 TB iCloud plan on a full 128 GB iPhone still leaves you with a full 128 GB iPhone.

Each one also has its own warning and its own consequences:

| | iPhone storage full | iCloud storage full |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where to check | **Settings → General → iPhone Storage** | **Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Storage** (or **Manage Account Storage**) |
| What happens | A storage alert, and Apple suggests removing content such as videos and apps | No iCloud Backups, no new uploads to iCloud Photos, iCloud Drive stops staying up to date, and iCloud Mail can't send or receive |
| The fix | Free up space on the phone | Free up iCloud space, or buy more |

Those consequences for a full iCloud come from Apple's [Manage your iCloud storage](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108922). Check both screens before you do anything, so you know which meter you're actually dealing with.

## The one way iCloud can free space on your iPhone

iCloud only takes load off your phone for Photos, and only if you ask it to. Apple's [Set up and use iCloud Photos](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108782) says photos and videos in iCloud Photos use **both** your iCloud storage and your device storage. What changes is how much of each.

With **Optimize Storage** on, your iPhone keeps space-saving versions of your photos and videos, and iCloud keeps the full-resolution originals. When the phone needs more room, iCloud Photos optimizes the library, starting with the photos and videos you open least. You can still download an original whenever you open it.

To turn it on:

1. Open **Settings** and tap your name.
2. Tap **iCloud**.
3. Under **Saved to iCloud**, tap **Photos**.
4. Make sure **Sync this device** is on.
5. On iOS 27, tap **iPhone Storage**, then choose **Optimize Storage**. On iOS 26 or earlier, just choose **Optimize Storage**.

Two things to know before you rely on it:

- **Your iCloud plan has to fit the whole library.** The originals now live in iCloud. If your library is bigger than your plan, new photos and videos stop uploading, and the phone can't hand them off.
- **It's gradual.** Apple ties it to the phone needing space, so don't expect the iPhone Storage bar to drop the moment you flip the switch.

## Already using Optimize Storage and still full?

This is where most forum threads end up. The phone still keeps a version of every photo and video, and those versions take room, so a large library still weighs something even when optimized. Photos is also only one entry in the list.

Open **Settings → General → iPhone Storage** and look at what's actually at the top. Apple's [storage page](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108429) lists the categories you'll see, such as Apps, Photos, Messages, and **iCloud Drive**, which counts files from iCloud Drive that you've downloaded to the phone. From there:

- **Apps:** tap one and choose **Offload App** to remove the app but keep its documents and data, or **Delete App** to remove both.
- **Messages:** if it's near the top, the photos and videos in your conversations are worth reviewing.
- **Recently Deleted:** photos you've deleted keep using phone space for up to 30 days. Here's [how to empty Recently Deleted in Photos, Files, and Messages](/guides/empty-recently-deleted-iphone).

Our full walkthrough for [what to delete first when iPhone storage is full](/guides/iphone-storage-full) covers the rest in order of how much space each usually frees.

## Why deleting photos to "save them in iCloud" backfires

A common move is to delete photos from the phone, assuming iCloud keeps a copy. With iCloud Photos on, it doesn't. Apple says photos and videos you delete on one device are deleted everywhere you use iCloud Photos, including in iCloud. Apple also notes that iCloud Photos isn't duplicated in your iCloud Backup, and recommends keeping a separate backup copy of your library.

So with iCloud Photos, there are only two ways to make the library take less room: delete what you truly don't want, or make what you keep **smaller**. Shrinking a large video, or [keeping a Live Photo as a still](/guides/live-photos-storage-iphone), cuts what the phone stores, and once the smaller version replaces the original in your synced library, the space in iCloud drops too. See [how to compress videos on iPhone](/guides/compress-videos-iphone) for the built-in ways to do that.

One more catch from Apple's iCloud page: if you're already **over** your iCloud storage limit, photos and videos you delete are removed immediately and skip Recently Deleted, so they can't be recovered. Be sure before you delete while iCloud is full.

## If it's iCloud that's full instead

If **Settings → [your name] → iCloud** is the screen showing a full bar, Apple's iCloud storage page suggests:

1. Check **Recommended for You** on the iCloud screen for quick wins.
2. Open **Storage** (or **Manage Account Storage**) → **Backups**, tap your device, and turn off apps you don't need backed up. You can also choose **Turn Off and Delete from iCloud** for a backup you no longer need.
3. Review the biggest items, which for most people are Photos and backups.

Items in Recently Deleted in Photos don't count toward your iCloud storage, so emptying it won't change the iCloud number, only the phone's.

## The short version

Your iPhone and iCloud have separate storage, and free space in one never fills the other. If the phone is full, turn on **Optimize Storage** for Photos if your iCloud plan can hold your library, then clear space on the phone itself. If you want both meters to go down, shrink your largest videos instead of deleting them.
