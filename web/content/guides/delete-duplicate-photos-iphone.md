Saving the same photo twice happens constantly: a picture someone sent you in two different chats, a screenshot you saved again, an image downloaded from the web more than once. Since iOS 16, the Photos app can find these for you.

## Use the built-in Duplicates album

1. Open the **Photos** app.
2. Scroll down to **Utilities**. In iOS 18 and later, it's one of the collections below your library.
3. Tap **Duplicates**.
4. Review each set. Tap **Merge** next to a set to combine it.
5. To do everything at once, tap **Select**, then **Select All**, then **Merge**.

### What Merge actually does

Merge keeps the **highest-quality version** and combines relevant details from the others, such as captions, keywords, and favorites, into that one copy. The extra copies move to **Recently Deleted**.

That last part matters: **the space isn't freed until you empty Recently Deleted.** Open **Utilities → Recently Deleted**, unlock it, and tap **Select → Delete All** when you're confident.

## What the Duplicates album won't find

Apple's tool looks for **exact duplicates** and copies that differ only in resolution or file format. It doesn't flag:

- **Burst sequences** and near-identical shots taken a second apart
- **Edited versions** saved as separate photos
- **Screenshots of the same thing** taken at different times

For bursts, open **Media Types → Bursts** in Photos, tap a burst, tap **Select**, choose the frames you like, and tap **Done**. iOS asks whether to keep only your favorites.

## Bigger wins than duplicates

Duplicates are satisfying to clean up, but they're often a few hundred megabytes. Two places usually hold far more:

- **Videos.** A single long 4K video can be larger than all your duplicates combined. See [what to delete first when storage is full](/guides/iphone-storage-full).
- **Screenshots.** Years of them add up. Here's [how to delete them in bulk](/guides/delete-all-screenshots-iphone).
