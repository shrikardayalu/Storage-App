Most long videos have a good 30 seconds in the middle of a lot of pocket, floor, and "is it recording?". Trimming is the quickest way to make them worth keeping. Whether it frees any space depends on which button you tap at the end.

## How to trim a video in the Photos app

These are the steps from Apple's guide to [trimming a video on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/trim-video-length-adjust-speed-and-edit-audio-iph1d1344a2d/ios):

1. Open **Photos** and tap the video.
2. Tap **Edit**.
3. Drag either end of the frame viewer below the video to set where it starts and stops. Tap **Play** to check it.
4. Tap **Done**.
5. Tap **Save Video** or **Save Video as New Clip**.

That's all trimming in Photos does: it moves the start and the end. It doesn't cut sections out of the middle.

## Save Video vs Save Video as New Clip

The two buttons look alike but leave your library in different states.

| | Save Video | Save Video as New Clip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What's in your library afterwards | The trimmed video only | The trimmed clip and the full-length original |
| Can you undo it later? | Yes: **Edit → Revert** | No, but the original is still there |
| Best for | Tidying a clip you might want back in full | Saving space, once you delete the original |

Apple says **Save Video** saves only the trimmed version. It also says you can undo the edit after saving: open the video, tap **Edit**, then tap **Revert**. Photos can only do that if it still has the full-length video to go back to, so don't count on **Save Video** to give you the space back.

A video saved as a new clip, on the other hand, can't be reverted to the original, Apple says. It's a separate, shorter video. Once you delete the full-length one, the short clip is all that's left.

## The trim that actually frees space

1. Trim the video as above and tap **Save Video as New Clip**.
2. Play the new clip to check you kept the right part.
3. Go back to the full-length original and tap the **Delete** button.
4. Empty **Recently Deleted**. Deleted videos are kept there for 30 days before Photos removes them for good, and they still take up space in the meantime. Here's [how to empty Recently Deleted on iPhone](/guides/empty-recently-deleted-iphone).

To see the difference, check **Settings → General → iPhone Storage** before you start and after you empty Recently Deleted. Apple's [storage page](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108429) shows how Photos and videos count in that list.

If you use iCloud Photos, deleting the original removes it from all your devices and from iCloud, so the trimmed clip is the one that's kept everywhere. That's the point, but it's also why step 2 matters.

## Which videos are worth trimming?

Long ones. Open **Photos**, tap **Collections**, scroll down to **Media Types**, and tap **Videos** to see them all in one place. The ones that pay off most are long recordings where you only care about a short moment: a concert, a kid's school show, a screen recording, a video you started too early.

Trimming helps less when the whole video matters. Then the size comes from how it was recorded, not how long it runs, and the fix is a smaller copy at a lower resolution. Here's [how to compress videos on iPhone](/guides/compress-videos-iphone) with the built-in Shortcuts app.

## Trimming a lot of videos

Photos trims one video at a time, and the space-saving route above is four steps per video plus Recently Deleted at the end. That's fine for a handful of clips. For a library with dozens of long recordings, do them in one sitting, delete the originals as you go, and empty Recently Deleted once at the end.

If your phone is full and you're not sure videos are the reason, start with [what to clear first when iPhone storage is full](/guides/iphone-storage-full), which walks through reading the storage list app by app.
