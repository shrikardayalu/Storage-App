## What Cleanup does well

Credit where it's due. Cleanup is genuinely broad:

- **Duplicates and similar shots**, grouped with a suggested best photo, plus large and "lookalike" videos.
- **Video compression**, so you can choose which videos to shrink.
- **Contacts and email cleanup**: merge duplicate contacts, remove incomplete ones, and clear promotional mail.
- **Extras** like a PIN-protected "Secret Space", home and lock screen widgets, and charging animations.

If you want one app that touches your photos, your contacts, and your inbox, Cleanup is built for that.

## Deleting vs. shrinking

Most of what fills an iPhone isn't junk. It's videos of your kids, concerts, trips, and thousands of Live Photos that each carry a hidden three-second video clip. A cleaner that only finds things to delete makes you choose between space and memories.

{app} starts by making things smaller:

- **4K videos** are re-saved at the same quality in a more efficient format, or at 1080p. The smaller copy keeps the original's date, location, and favorite status, and {app} won't replace a video unless the copy is meaningfully smaller.
- **Long recordings** can be trimmed to the part you want before they're shrunk.
- **Live Photos** become stills. The photo looks the same; only the hidden video goes.
- **Screenshots**, which iPhones save as large PNG files, are re-saved as smaller HEIC copies with the same pixels.

Cleanup does compress videos, which is a real plus. But it doesn't advertise trimming, Live Photo conversion, or screenshot re-saving, and those are often where the easy gigabytes are. See [how much space Live Photos take](/guides/live-photos-storage-iphone) and [how to compress videos on iPhone](/guides/compress-videos-iphone) for why.

## What you'll actually pay

Cleanup's App Store listing offers a **7-day free trial** that renews automatically, and its in-app purchases list nine different **weekly** prices, from $5.95 up to $11.99, plus one **$29.99 yearly** plan. Which one you see depends on the offer the app shows you. At $11.99 a week, a year comes to more than $600, a sum at least one App Store reviewer has pointed out.

{app} works differently:

- **No trial at all**, so there's nothing to remember to cancel.
- **The free version is genuinely usable.** You can scan, swipe through likely deletes, keep the best shot of each burst, clean up big files, and follow the step-by-step guides, with no daily cap and no ads.
- **Pro is {weekly} a week or {annual} a year**, and the scan shows you how much Pro would free on *your* phone before you decide.

## Privacy

Both apps say your photos are analyzed on your iPhone. The difference is in what else leaves it.

Cleanup's App Store privacy label lists **purchases, identifiers, and usage data as "Data Used to Track You"**, and an independent network test in January 2025 found it talking to advertising and analytics services. That's common for free-with-ads apps, but it's worth knowing before you grant access to your whole photo library.

{app} has no account, no ads, and no analytics, and it makes no network connections at all. Its privacy label is **Data Not Collected**. Our guide to [whether iPhone cleaner apps are safe](/guides/are-iphone-cleaner-apps-safe) explains how to read any app's label.

## Things {app} does that Cleanup doesn't

- **Measures the result.** {app} reads your free space before and after every cleanup, including after you empty Recently Deleted, so the "you freed" figure is what really came back, not an estimate.
- **Reminds you about Recently Deleted.** Deleted and replaced photos sit there for 30 days and still take up space. {app} walks you through emptying it, the step most people miss when they [delete duplicate photos](/guides/delete-duplicate-photos-iphone).
- **Helps with what no app can touch.** iOS doesn't let any app clear WhatsApp, Messages, or Safari data, so {app} gives you short, drawn step-by-step guides and measures the space each one frees.
- **Cleans up the Files app.** Pick a folder and it finds large files, duplicate copies, and old downloads.

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Cleanup if** you want contacts, email, a photo vault, and widgets in one app, and you're comfortable managing a weekly subscription.

**Choose {app} if** most of your storage is photos and videos and you'd rather keep them smaller than delete them, if you want to see exactly how much space came back, or if a clean privacy label matters to you. The free scan takes a minute and shows the number before you commit to anything.
