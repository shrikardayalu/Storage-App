## What Cleaner Kit does well

Cleaner Kit is an everything app, and it's updated constantly:

- **A deep toolbox**: duplicates, similar and blurry photos, similar videos, 4K video and screen recording categories, video compression, and Live Photos to stills.
- **Beyond photos**: merge and back up contacts, and bulk-clean email by sender, date, or size.
- **A secret folder** locked with Face ID or a passcode, and favorites that protect photos from deletion.
- **Cleanup Statistics** that keep a running total of what you've deleted since day one.

## The free version, and the ads

Cleaner Kit's App Store listing says it **contains advertising** and promises "essential cleanup tools at no cost", without saying which tools or how much. Full access is a subscription billed weekly or yearly, and the in-app purchase list shows ten different prices between $4.99 and $9.99, without the plan lengths. Reviewers commonly describe limits on how much you can delete for free.

{app} has **no ads at all**, and its free tools have **no daily cap**:

- **Swipe to clean** shows extra copies, blurry and black shots, receipts, documents, and old screenshots, biggest first. Swipe left to delete, right to keep, with undo.
- **Best shot of each burst**, the **Files cleanup**, and every step-by-step guide are free too.

When you want the heavy lifting done in one tap, **Pro is {annual} a year**, or {weekly} for a single week.

## Keeping more, deleting less

Both apps can compress videos and turn Live Photos into stills, so on the basics they're even. {app} adds two tools that matter if you'd rather not delete:

- **Trimming.** Cut a long recording down to the moment you care about, then shrink it.
- **Screenshot re-saving.** iPhone screenshots are saved as large PNG files. {app} re-saves the ones you want to keep as HEIC copies at a fraction of the size, with the same pixels.

Every shrunk video and converted photo keeps its date, location, favorite status, and album. The original sits in **Recently Deleted** for 30 days, so you can compare and change your mind.

## Privacy

Both apps say photo analysis happens on your iPhone. But Cleaner Kit is ad-supported, and its App Store privacy label lists a long set of **data used to track you**, including **location, identifiers, usage data, and purchases**. Its privacy policy confirms it uses advertising and analytics tools.

{app} has no ads, no analytics, and no network connections. Its label is **Data Not Collected**. For a plain-English rundown of what these labels mean, see [are iPhone cleaner apps safe](/guides/are-iphone-cleaner-apps-safe).

## Measured, not estimated

Cleaner Kit's statistics add up what you've deleted. {app} goes a step further and reads your iPhone's actual free space before and after every action, including after you empty Recently Deleted, and shows you the real change next to its own count. If you've ever deleted a pile of photos and watched your storage barely move, [this is usually why](/guides/iphone-storage-full).

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Cleaner Kit if** you want contacts, email, a secret folder, and photo cleanup all in one app, and ads don't bother you.

**Choose {app} if** you want an ad-free cleaner with a clean privacy label, if your storage is mostly videos and screenshots you'd rather shrink than delete, or if you want proof of the space you got back.
