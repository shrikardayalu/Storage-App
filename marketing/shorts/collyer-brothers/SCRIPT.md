# The Trap That Caught Its Maker (Collyer brothers), script v1 draft (2026-11-27)

Format: ~60 s (~230 words) vertical cartoon short, ~3.7 words/s, one steady deadpan narrator.
Tone: dark and gripping, never jokey about the deaths (no `<chuckle>` anywhere).
Ending: callback twist (Langley saved every paper for a day that never came; your phone saves photos for "someday"),
with a light plug in the last ~5 s.
Status: **draft, waiting for approval and a hook pick.**

## Facts and sources
- Homer (born 1881) and Langley (born 1885) Collyer lived at 2078 Fifth Avenue, at 128th Street in Harlem, in a four-story brownstone. Both went to Columbia. Homer was an admiralty lawyer. Langley studied engineering and chemistry and was a concert pianist who played Carnegie Hall. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collyer_brothers))
- Homer went blind in 1933 (hemorrhages in his eyes), then was paralyzed by inflammatory rheumatism. Langley cared for him. He fed him "one hundred oranges a week, black bread, and peanut butter," which he claimed was curing the blindness. He saved newspapers so Homer could "catch up on the news" when his sight came back. (Wikipedia, [All That's Interesting](https://allthatsinteresting.com/colly))
- The phone was cut off in 1937. Electricity, water and gas were turned off in 1938 for unpaid bills. (Wikipedia)
- After rumors of cash and valuables inside, Langley built tunnels through the junk and rigged them with tripwires and booby traps "to exclude burglars." (Wikipedia, [Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/curious-fact-of-the-week-collyer-brothers))
- March 21, 1947: an anonymous caller reported a dead body. Police got in through a second-floor window, and digging to Homer took about five hours. He had been dead about ten hours. The cause was starvation and heart disease. (Wikipedia, [Amusing Planet](https://amusingplanet.com/2018/09/the-collyer-brothers-historys-worst.html))
- April 8, 1947 (18 days later): a workman found Langley about ten feet from where Homer died, in a two-foot-wide tunnel. One of his own traps had crushed him under bundles of newspapers. He was apparently bringing food to Homer and had been dead since around March 9. So Homer, blind and paralyzed, was alone for about twelve days. Rats had eaten part of the body. (Wikipedia, [NYT 1947 via barrypopik.com](https://barrypopik.com/florida/entry/New/P36115))
- What came out of the house: fourteen pianos, more than 25,000 books, pickled human organs in jars, the chassis of an old Model T, a horse's jawbone and an early X-ray machine. (Wikipedia)
- The tonnage varies by source: Wikipedia's lead says more than 140 tons, its body and other sources say about 120 (some say 100). **The script says "over a hundred tons"**, which every source supports. Change it to "a hundred and forty" only if you're OK with the contested figure.
- The house was razed in July 1947 as unsafe and a fire hazard. The lot is now a small park. (Wikipedia)
- "Collyer's mansion" is a firefighting term for a home packed so full it's dangerous to enter. ([Fire Engineering](https://fireengineering.com/firefighting/sheridan-collyers-mansions), [FireRescue1](https://www.firerescue1.com/firefighting-history/inside-the-collyer-mansion-how-extreme-hoarding-sparked-a-warning-phrase-for-firefighters))
- "Thousands of photos saved for someday" is an everyday line, not a statistic.
- App line: "cleans up gigabytes in minutes" matches the backed claim in `docs/PRICING.md`.

## Script (with hook A)
```
A man booby-trapped his whole house to keep out burglars. <short pause> The trap caught him instead.
And upstairs, his blind brother was still waiting for dinner.
Harlem, New York. Homer Collyer was a lawyer. His little brother Langley played piano at Carnegie Hall.
Then Homer went blind, and Langley stayed home to look after him.
He fed him a hundred oranges a week, sure it would bring his sight back.
And he saved every newspaper, so Homer could catch up on the news the day he could see again.
The bills went unpaid. The phone, the lights, the water and the gas were all cut off. The piles kept growing.
Fourteen pianos. Twenty-five thousand books. Pickled organs in jars. Most of an old Model T.
People said there was cash hidden inside, so Langley dug tunnels through the junk and strung them with tripwires.
Then one day, somebody called the police. There was a dead man in the house.
It took five hours to dig to him. It was Homer. He had starved.
Langley was nowhere. People said he'd run away.
Eighteen days later, workers found him ten feet from his brother, crushed under his own trap, on his way to bring Homer food.
They hauled more than a hundred tons out of that house.
Firefighters still call a home packed like that a Collyer mansion.
<short pause> Langley saved every page for a day that never came.
Your phone does it too. Thousands of photos, saved for someday.
SimpleShrink cleans up gigabytes in minutes. Link in bio.
```
About 235 words, roughly 63 s at 3.7 words/s. Three tags.

## Hook options (2026-11-27, pick one)
Each option replaces only the first one or two lines. The rest of the script stays except where noted.

**A. Irony, reason hidden (recommended).** Title: "The Trap That Caught Its Maker".
```
A man booby-trapped his whole house to keep out burglars. <short pause> The trap caught him instead.
And upstairs, his blind brother was still waiting for dinner.
```
First frame: a low, wide shot inside a tunnel of newspaper bundles. A hand strings a tripwire across it, and the
camera pushes along the wire. Why it wins: the irony fits in one sentence, it shows a strange action, and the second
line makes it worse while leaving the real question open (what happened to the brother?). The ending answers it.
Changes: none, the script is written for it.

**B. Discovery, missing brother.** Title: "Five Hours to Reach a Dead Man".
```
Police needed five hours to dig through one New York house to reach a dead man.
His brother lived there too. <short pause> Nobody could find him.
```
First frame: police passing bundles out of a second-floor window, junk spilling onto the sidewalk. Changes: the story
becomes a flashback, so "Then one day, somebody called the police..." and "It took five hours..." shrink to "Back to
that phone call." and the ending reveal ("ten feet from his brother") does more work. The mystery is very gripping,
but the flashback makes the middle harder to follow.

**C. Love, then horror.** Title: "He Saved Every Newspaper for His Blind Brother".
```
A man saved every newspaper in New York for his blind brother, <short pause> for the day he'd see again.
It ended with a hundred tons of paper, and two bodies.
```
First frame: a man stacking a fresh bundle onto a wall of newspapers in candlelight. Changes: the "And he saved every
newspaper..." line is cut so it isn't said twice. It loops best with the last line, but the second line gives away
both deaths, so it holds people less well.

**D. Strange inventory.** Title: "What Police Found Inside 2078 Fifth Avenue".
```
Inside one New York townhouse, police found fourteen pianos, a car, <short pause> and pickled organs in jars.
They also found the man who lived there. Eventually.
```
First frame: a flashlight beam crossing a piano lid stacked with jars. Changes: the "Fourteen pianos..." line goes.
It's a strong curiosity hook, but "a car" stretches the truth (it was a chassis), so it would need to be "half a car".

**E. Oranges.** Title: "A Hundred Oranges a Week".
```
A man fed his blind brother a hundred oranges a week, sure it would cure him.
That was the most normal thing in the house.
```
First frame: oranges rolling down a hallway of junk. Changes: the oranges line in the middle goes. It's the oddest
detail, but it hides the darkness, so it suits a lighter edit more than this one.

## Shot ideas (rough, for the build)
- "A man booby-trapped his whole house...": a tunnel of newspaper bundles, Langley's hand stringing a wire, and a heavy stack balanced above it. The camera pushes along the wire.
- "And upstairs, his blind brother...": a crane up through the floor to Homer in a chair, eyes closed, a candle, waiting.
- "Harlem, New York...": a whip to a sunny 1900s Fifth Avenue brownstone, the brothers young and smart on the stoop, with a piano heard through a window.
- "Then Homer went blind...": the light inside drains to candle warmth. Langley sets down a tray.
- "A hundred oranges a week": a crate tipping, oranges rolling across the floor, with Langley peeling one.
- "He saved every newspaper...": Langley adds a bundle to a growing wall. The camera trucks along stacks that rise to the ceiling.
- "The bills went unpaid...": a meter dial stopping, a tap dripping dry, the candle the only light.
- "Fourteen pianos...": a fast dolly through the hoard: pianos stacked, a wall of books, jars glowing faintly, a Model T chassis wedged in a room.
- "People said there was cash hidden...": kids peering in the window. Langley crawls a two-foot tunnel and ties a tripwire.
- "Then one day...": an exterior night shot, police at the door, one climbing to a second-floor window.
- "It took five hours...": bundles passed hand to hand out the window, and a crowd on the sidewalk.
- "Langley was nowhere...": newspapers on a stand with question marks (no readable headlines, since captions are on).
- "Eighteen days later...": a slow push down the tunnel to a fallen stack. Only a hand and a dropped orange show. Nothing gory.
- "They hauled more than a hundred tons...": a crane-out over the house, trucks piled high, the house going grey.
- "Firefighters still call...": a modern firefighter's helmet light sweeping a cluttered hallway.
- Callback and plug: a single bundle of yellowed papers tied with string dissolves into a phone's photo grid, then the real picker and results screens on a phone, with the SimpleShrink icon clear of it. End.
