# The Squirrel's Fake Hole (squirrel nuts), script v1 draft (2026-11-27)

Format: ~60 s (~230 words) vertical cartoon short, ~3.7 words/s, one steady deadpan narrator.
Ending: callback twist (a squirrel's forgotten nut grows into a tree; your forgotten files just sit there), with a
light plug in the last ~4 s.
Status: **approved 2026-11-27 with hook A** (the script below). Scratch cut built; waiting on the real narration
(VOICE_SCRIPT.md, build steps in NOTES.md).

## A correction to the idea
"Squirrels forget most of their nuts" is popular but weak. The "up to 74 % never recovered" figure is repeated
everywhere, but I couldn't trace it to a paper. A 1980 urban study puts recovery at about 85 %, and memory experiments
show squirrels are very good at it. So the script turns the myth into the twist: squirrels are memory geniuses, and the
forest still comes from the nuts they never came back for (because they found enough food, or a hawk got them). The
callback lands the same way.

## Facts and sources
- A gray squirrel can bury up to about 3,000 nuts in a season, one per hole (scatter-hoarding). ([Scientific American](https://scientificamerican.com/article/do-squirrels-remember-where-they-buried-their-nuts), [Scienceblog](https://scienceblog.com/d-a-grey-squirrel-buries-several-thousand-nuts-in-a-season-and-never-comes-back-for-a-good-share-of-them-which-is-how-an-acorn-ends-up-planted-at-the-right-depth-in-open-ground-a-fair-part-of-the-oak/)). The script says "up to three thousand".
- Deceptive caching: when other squirrels are watching, gray squirrels dig a hole, mime burying, cover it, and leave with the nut. It happened in about 13 % of caching events (Steele et al. 2008, *Animal Behaviour*; about 1 in 8). Steele called it tactical deception, once thought to be a primate thing. ([National Wildlife Federation 2008](https://www.nwf.org/Magazines/National-Wildlife/2008/Science-Sleuths-How-Squirrels-Hide-Nuts), [Northern Woodlands](https://northernwoodlands.org/discoveries/sneaky_squirrels))
- Fox squirrels "chunk" their caches, sorting nuts by type when they collect them from one spot (Delgado & Jacobs 2017, *Royal Society Open Science*; about 45 squirrels on the UC Berkeley campus). ([Berkeley News](https://news.berkeley.edu/2017/09/12/nut-chunking/), [paper PDF](https://jacobs.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Delgado-2017-Roy-Soc-Open-Sci.pdf))
- Squirrels cut out the embryo of white oak acorns (which sprout in the fall) before caching them, so they can't germinate and keep for months. Red oak acorns close to sprouting get the same treatment. (Steele et al. 2001; Smallwood, Steele & Faeth 2001; [PMC review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3894888/), [Smithsonian Magazine 2025](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/blogs/smithsonian-books/2025/05/22/inside-the-secret-lives-of-squirrels-and-their-half-eaten-acorns/))
- Recovery: a 1980 urban study estimated gray squirrels get back about 85 % of their caches. ([Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/science/how-squirrels-find-nuts/)). Britannica: some nuts are surely never dug up, but it's unclear whether they're forgotten or just not needed. ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/story/is-it-true-that-squirrels-forget-where-they-bury-about-half-of-their-food))
- Nuts that are never dug up are already planted at a good depth, and this is a real way oaks spread (jays do it too, so the script says "a lot of oak forests", not "all"). (Scienceblog above, [Smithsonian Science Education Center](https://ssec.si.edu/stemvisions-blog/why-do-squirrels-bury-nuts-and-other-mysteries))
- App line: "cleans up gigabytes in minutes" matches the backed claim in `docs/PRICING.md`.

## Script (with hook A)
```
A squirrel will dig a hole, pretend to drop a nut in, <short pause> and walk off with the nut still in its mouth.
It's lying. On purpose.
Every fall, one gray squirrel buries up to three thousand nuts, one hole at a time, all over its patch of woods.
And every other squirrel is watching, waiting to dig them up.
So when it knows it has an audience, it fakes it. Scientists caught them faking about one burial in eight.
Their memory is ridiculous too. Fox squirrels sort their nuts by type, like a filing system under the lawn.
And if an acorn is about to sprout, a squirrel bites out the tiny part that would grow, so its snack stays a snack all winter.
People love to say squirrels forget where their nuts are. Mostly, they don't. One study found city squirrels dug up about eighty-five percent.
But not all of them. Some squirrels find enough food somewhere else. Some get caught by a hawk before spring.
And the nuts nobody comes back for are already planted, at just the right depth, far from the parent tree.
That's how a lot of oak forests spread. <short pause> Not from the nuts squirrels remembered. From the ones they left behind.
A squirrel's forgotten nut turns into a tree.
Your forgotten files just sit there. <chuckle> They don't grow into anything.
SimpleShrink cleans up gigabytes in minutes. Link in bio.
```

## Hook options (2026-11-27, pick one)
Each option replaces only the first one or two lines; the rest of the script stays except where noted.

**A. Strange action, reason hidden (recommended).** Title: "Why Squirrels Fake-Bury Their Nuts".
```
A squirrel will dig a hole, pretend to drop a nut in, <short pause> and walk off with the nut still in its mouth.
It's lying. On purpose.
```
First frame: low and wide on the squirrel already digging, paws flinging dirt, nut in its mouth. Why it wins: it's the
most visual, the strangest, and it opens a clear question (why lie?) without giving away the forest. Changes: none,
the script is written for it.

**B. Myth flip.** Title: "Squirrels Don't Forget Their Nuts".
```
Everyone says squirrels forget where they bury their nuts. <short pause> They don't.
So how did they plant half the oaks around you?
```
First frame: a squirrel staring straight at a patch of lawn, then digging up a nut on the first try. Changes: the
"People love to say..." line becomes "Mostly, they find them. One study found city squirrels dug up about eighty-five
percent." The fake-burial beat moves after the memory beat. "Half the oaks" is too strong for the sources; it would
need to be "so many oaks".

**C. Surgery.** Title: "The Squirrel That Operates on Acorns".
```
Before it buries an acorn, a squirrel sometimes bites out the one part that could grow into a tree.
It's not being mean. <short pause> It's planning.
```
First frame: extreme close-up, the squirrel turning an acorn and nipping one end. Changes: the embryo line in the
middle is cut so it isn't told twice. Strong and strange, but harder to read at a glance than a fake hole.

**D. Number.** Title: "3,000 Nuts and a Perfect Memory".
```
One squirrel can bury three thousand nuts in a single fall, <short pause> and remember where most of them are.
The ones it doesn't are why you have trees.
```
First frame: a time-lapse of holes popping up across a lawn as the squirrel zips between them. Changes: the
"Every fall..." line goes. The second line gives away the payoff, so it's the weakest at keeping people watching.

**E. Irony.** Title: "The Forest a Squirrel Never Came Back For".
```
Some of the oak trees around you were planted by a squirrel, <short pause> and it never came back.
Not because it forgot.
```
First frame: low camera rising up a huge oak trunk, a tiny squirrel at its base. Changes: the "People love to say..."
line is trimmed, since the hook already raises the myth. Pretty and loops well, but the first frame is slow.

## Shot ideas (rough, for the build)
- "A squirrel will dig a hole...": Low, wide, autumn park lawn. The squirrel digs, mimes the drop, pats the dirt, and trots off with the nut still in its cheeks. Camera trucks with it.
- "It's lying. On purpose.": It glances back over its shoulder at the camera. Beat.
- "Every fall, one gray squirrel...": Crane up: dozens of small dirt mounds across the lawn, falling leaves.
- "And every other squirrel is watching...": Rack across a branch: three other squirrels peeking out from behind trunks.
- "So when it knows it has an audience...": The hero squirrel turns its back to the watchers, fakes a hole; a watcher digs it up later and finds nothing.
- "Their memory is ridiculous too...": Cutaway under the lawn: rows of nuts sorted by type in neat little drawers (the filing-system gag).
- "And if an acorn is about to sprout...": Close-up, a tiny green sprout tip on an acorn; the squirrel nips it off and tucks the acorn away.
- "People love to say...": The squirrel digs straight down and pops up with a nut, first try, every time (three quick tries).
- "But not all of them...": Winter light. A hawk shadow sweeps the lawn; one squirrel full and asleep in a nest.
- "And the nuts nobody comes back for...": Under the snow, one acorn left in the ground; spring, a sprout pushes up.
- "That's how a lot of oak forests spread...": Time-lapse: the sprout becomes a sapling, then a grove, camera rising with it into a full oak forest (the one glow moment).
- Callback and plug: A forgotten acorn sprouting beside a phone photo grid of old files that just sit there; then the real picker/results screens on a phone, SimpleShrink icon clear of it, end.
