# America's Attic: voice script for Gemini TTS

Paste the **Style** into the Style field, and the script block into one speech block. Tags in angle brackets are
Gemini's inline expression tags; everything else is read word for word. Numbers are spelled out so they're read right.

## Speaker and style
- **Voice:** Charon, as in the Geniza short, so the account has one narrator. Audition Algieba or Iapetus on the first
  line only if Charon sounds off on this script.
- **Style:**
  > Warm, curious male storyteller narrating a short animated history video. Fast and steady, about 220 words a minute,
  > with almost no pauses between sentences. Lightly amused, deadpan on the jokes, never dramatic or announcer-like.
  > Keep the same tone and pace all the way through, including the last two lines.

## Script
```
The world's biggest museum owns over a hundred and fifty million things, and you will never see ninety-nine percent of them.
The Smithsonian only puts about one percent on display. The rest lives in Maryland, in warehouses the size of football fields.
In one, a whale's spine sits on a shelf like a spare part.
Down the road, space suits lie on racks, <short pause> like astronauts who never left.
Thirty-five million bugs sleep in over a hundred thousand drawers.
And one room holds twenty-five million animals floating in jars, in half a million gallons of alcohol. <short pause> Nobody lights a candle in there.
So why keep a drawer full of dead birds nobody looks at?
Because someone might need it someday.
In two thousand nine, someone did. A plane hit a flock of birds over New York, lost both engines, and landed on the Hudson River.
Investigators scraped bird mush out of the engines and mailed it to the Smithsonian.
Scientists matched the feathers and DNA against those dead birds in drawers. Canada geese, migrating from up north.
So the museum's junk was never junk. Every drawer is labeled, and somebody knows exactly what's in it.
Your camera roll is the same museum. Maybe thirty thousand photos, no labels, <chuckle> and you only ever look at the last twenty.
SimpleShrink cleans up gigabytes in minutes. Link in bio.
```
