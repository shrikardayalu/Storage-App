## Facts and sources
- The Smithsonian is usually called the world's largest museum, education and research complex. It holds about 155 million objects and specimens (the script says "over 150 million"). ([Smithsonian factsheet: Collections](https://newsdesk.si.edu/factsheets/smithsonian-collections), [SI Archives: how many objects](https://siarchives.si.edu/blog/how-many-objects-does-smithsonian-have))
- Only about 1 % is on display at any time (the factsheet says "less than 1 %"). (Collections factsheet, [Nat Geo: inside the secret storerooms](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/a-rare-look-inside-the-smithsonians-secret-storerooms))
- Most of the reserve lives at the Museum Support Center in Suitland, Maryland: five pods, each about the size of a football field and ~28 ft tall. ([Smithsonian factsheet: Museum Support Center](https://newsdesk.si.edu/factsheets/museum-support-center), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Museum_Support_Center))
- Whale skeletons are stored there; a TV crew filmed a whole whale spine on a tall yellow shelving unit. ([WJLA: the museum behind the museum](https://wjla.com/news/local/smithsonian-museum-support-center-suitland-whale-bones-baleen-antarctic-meteorites-meteorite-collection-conservation), [NMNH: MSC](https://naturalhistory.si.edu/research/msc))
- The "wet collection" pod holds about 25 million specimens preserved in fluids such as alcohol and formalin, in 500,000+ gallons of ethanol. (MSC factsheet, [WTOP 2026](https://wtop.com/matt-about-town/2026/01/exploring-smithsonian-museum-of-natural-historys-entire-reserve-specimen-collection))
- The insect collection is 35+ million specimens in over 132,000 drawers. ([NMNH Entomology](https://www.naturalhistory.si.edu/research/entomology/about), [Wikipedia: Dept. of Entomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Entomology,_National_Museum_of_Natural_History))
- Air and Space holds about 270 complete pressure suits; most of the stored ones lay on racks at the Paul E. Garber Facility, also in Suitland. ([Air and Space Quarterly, 2024](https://www.airandspace.si.edu/air-and-space-quarterly/issue-10/spacesuits), [Smithsonian mag: the spacesuit morgue](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smithsonian-institution/air-and-space-museum-the-spacesuit-morgue-38463663/)). The script says "down the road" rather than "next to", because they're in different buildings.
- January 2009: US Airways Flight 1549 lost both engines to birds and landed on the Hudson. The Smithsonian's Feather Identification Lab compared feathers and DNA from the remains against museum specimens and identified Canada geese. A follow-up study (with Labrador goose specimens from the Field Museum) showed they were migratory birds from the north, not local geese. ([Smithsonian release](https://dashboard.si.edu/newsdesk/releases/scientists-determine-geese-involved-hudson-river-plane-crash-were-migratory), [Smithsonian mag](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/migratory-canada-geese-brought-down-flight-1549-12575190/), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2009/02/12/100643482/ntsb-canada-geese-caused-hudson-splashdown))
- "30,000 photos, you only look at the last 20" is an everyday joke, not a statistic, so it's phrased as "maybe".
- App line: "cleans up gigabytes in minutes" matches the backed claim in `docs/PRICING.md` ("frees up gigabytes of storage in minutes").

## Script
```
The world's biggest museum owns over a hundred and fifty million things, and you will never see ninety-nine percent of them.
The Smithsonian only puts about one percent on display. The rest lives in Maryland, in warehouses the size of football fields.
In one, a whale's spine sits on a shelf like a spare part.
Down the road, space suits lie on racks, <short pause> like astronauts who never left.
Thirty-five million bugs sleep in over a hundred thousand drawers.
And one room holds twenty-five million animals floating in jars, in half a million gallons of alcohol. <short pause> Nobody lights a candle in there.
So why keep a drawer full of dead birds nobody looks at?
Because someone might need it someday.
In two thousand nine, someone did. A plane hit a flock of birds over New York, lost both engines, and landed on the Hudson River.
Investigators scraped bird mush out of the engines and mailed it to the Smithsonian.
Scientists matched the feathers and DNA against those dead birds in drawers. Canada geese, migrating from up north.
So the museum's junk was never junk. Every drawer is labeled, and somebody knows exactly what's in it.
Your camera roll is the same museum. Maybe thirty thousand photos, no labels, <chuckle> and you only ever look at the last twenty.
SimpleShrink cleans up gigabytes in minutes. Link in bio.
```

## Shot ideas (rough, for the build)
- "The world's biggest museum...": Low dolly past a grand museum hall with a few glowing cases, then the floor drops away to reveal a huge warehouse beneath. White flash after the hook.
- "The Smithsonian only puts...": Blurred whip to a crane shot rising over endless repeating storage pods in a Maryland field (the reusable warehouse kit: boxy pods, tall shelving, rolling cabinets).
- "In one, a whale's spine..." to "Nobody lights a candle": The tour, one move per item. A curator walks under a whale spine on a yellow rack. Space suits on steel racks, with one glove that seems to twitch. Drawers sliding open one after another down a long aisle, bugs pinned inside. A dim blue room of jars with a tiny NO FLAMES sign, and a character blowing out a match.
- "So why keep a drawer..." and "someday": The curator pulls open a drawer of birds and shrugs at the camera: "someday".
- "In two thousand nine..." to "Canada geese": Night-blue sky over a cartoon New York, a flock crossing a plane, the plane gliding onto the river. A sealed bag in the mail, then the feather lab: a scientist with a microscope and a drawer of geese, and a match glow on one feather (the one "spark" moment of the video).
- "So the museum's junk...": Pull back down the aisle, with labels on every drawer lighting up in sequence.
- Camera roll line and plug: The warehouse shelves flatten into a phone's photo grid, the same rows in a new shape, with only the bottom row lit. The real picker/results screens on a phone, the SimpleShrink icon clear of it, and the video ends.
