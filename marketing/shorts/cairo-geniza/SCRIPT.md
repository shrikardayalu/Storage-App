## Facts and sources
- The Ben Ezra Synagogue in Fustat (Old Cairo) kept a genizah, a storeroom for worn-out writings. Hebrew script was treated as the writing of God, so texts in it could not be destroyed. The normal practice elsewhere was to empty a genizah from time to time and bury the contents in a cemetery. Cairo's stayed in use until Western scholars emptied it in the 1890s. ([Wikipedia: Cairo Geniza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_Geniza))
- By the 1890s it was a room with no windows or door, reached only by a ladder up to an opening in the wall. (Cairo Geniza)
- The material spans about the 6th to 19th centuries, mostly the 10th to 13th, and is about 400,000 fragments in all (counts vary). (Cairo Geniza)
- It wasn't only holy texts: there were legal documents, private letters, school exercises, merchants' account books, prescriptions and physicians' notes, and payments of day wages to workers. (Cairo Geniza)
- Children's alphabet practice sheets with doodles, including a roughly 1,000-year-old stick-man drawing (T-S AS 221.204). ([Cambridge: Discarded History, child's doodle](https://exhibitions.lib.cam.ac.uk/discardedhistory/artifacts/childs-doodle), [Bored with lessons?](https://exhibitions.lib.cam.ac.uk/ywa/?p=6))
- In May 1896 the Scottish twins Agnes Lewis and Margaret Gibson showed Solomon Schechter at Cambridge some leaves they brought back from Egypt. One was the Hebrew text of Ben Sira (Sirach), which had been known for centuries only in Greek and Latin. Schechter reached Cairo in December 1896 and removed most of the chamber's contents. ([Wikipedia: Solomon Schechter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Schechter), Cairo Geniza)
- The pages were covered in dust, full of insects, mangled and sometimes glued to each other. (Cairo Geniza)
- Schechter's haul: "over 100,000 pages" (Schechter article). Cambridge now holds nearly 193,000 fragments. The script says "over a hundred thousand".
- Scholars are still working on it: the Princeton Geniza Lab has been at it since 1986, and volunteers sort scans online. (Cairo Geniza)
- App line: "frees up gigabytes of storage in minutes" is backed by the owner's own test. Shrinking and compressing the videos on their library saved 30+ GB (see "Marketing claims" in `docs/PRICING.md`).

## Script
1. For almost a thousand years, one synagogue in Cairo refused to throw away a single piece of paper.
2. High up in its wall, there's a room with no door and no windows, just a hole you reach with a ladder.
3. Worn-out prayer books get shoved through that hole. Then letters, contracts, doctor's notes, a merchant's account books.
4. Even a bored kid's alphabet homework goes in, stick-man doodle and all.
5. And the pile just keeps growing.
6. So why would anyone keep a kid's doodle for a thousand years?
7. You see, Hebrew letters were treated as the writing of God, so anything written in them couldn't be destroyed.
8. Most places buried old pages every so often. This room never got emptied.
9. Then in 1896, two Scottish twins show a rabbi at Cambridge a few scraps from Cairo.
10. One is a lost book the world had only ever read in Greek and Latin.
11. So he heads to Cairo, climbs in through the dust and bugs, and hauls out over a hundred thousand pages.
12. Scholars are still sorting it, and most of it isn't holy at all. It's bills, wages and paperwork nobody meant to keep.
13. Turns out, if you never throw anything away, you don't get a library. You get a junk drawer with a few treasures in it.
14. Your phone works the same way. Nothing gets thrown out, it just piles up in a room you never open.
15. SimpleShrink finds the junk and frees up gigabytes of storage in minutes. Link in bio.
