# The Nanny's Boxes (Vivian Maier), script v1 (2026-11-26)

Format: ~60 s (~225 words) vertical cartoon short, ~3.7 words/s, one steady deadpan narrator.
Ending: callback twist (her best shots sat buried in a box; yours sit under screenshots), with a light plug in the last ~4 s.
Status: **draft, waiting on approval.**

## Facts and sources
- In 2007 Maier fell behind on payments for a storage unit on Chicago's North Side, and its contents (negatives, prints, recordings, 8 mm film) were auctioned. John Maloof bought a box of negatives and undeveloped film for about $400 (one blog says $380; most sources say $400, so the script says "four hundred"). ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivian_Maier), [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/1804651), [vivianmaier.com: history](https://www.vivianmaier.com/about-vivian-maier/history))
- Maloof was looking for old photos for a history book about his Chicago neighborhood (Portage Park). (Wikipedia, [Mother Jones 2011](https://www.motherjones.com/media/2011/05/vivian-maier-john-maloof/))
- Many rolls had never been developed; Maloof soon owned about 1,000 rolls of her film, and eventually more than 100,000 negatives (her official site says 100,000-150,000). ([Huxley-Parlour: the discovery](https://huxleyparlour.com/the-discovery-of-vivian-maier/), vivianmaier.com)
- She worked about 40 years as a nanny, mostly in Chicago and its North Shore suburbs; she answered the Gensburg family's ad in 1956. Her street work shows the city in the 1950s and 60s, with many self-portraits in reflections and shadows. (Wikipedia, [PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/arts/from-streets-scenes-of-the-50s-and-60s-to-neighborhood-characters-photographer-vivian-maier-captured-it-all))
- Former employers described her room stacked to the ceiling with newspapers, heavy enough that the floor sagged. (Wikipedia, [Newsweek](https://newsweek.com/vivian-maier-created-art-secret-more-50-years-224590))
- She died in April 2009. When Maloof googled her name, he found a death notice placed only days before. (Wikipedia, [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/photographers-talent-went-unknown-until-death/))
- He posted photos online in 2009 and they spread fast; "Finding Vivian Maier" (2013) was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature. (Wikipedia)
- "A great photo under four thousand screenshots" is an everyday joke, not a statistic.
- App line: "cleans up gigabytes in minutes" matches the backed claim in `docs/PRICING.md`.

## Script
```
In two thousand seven, a man paid four hundred dollars for a box of junk at a Chicago auction. <short pause> It turned out to be one of the great photo finds of the century.
The box came from a storage locker. Its owner had stopped paying the rent.
Inside were negatives. Thousands of them. Some rolls had never even been developed.
John Maloof was writing a history book about his neighborhood. He just wanted old pictures of buildings.
Instead he found Chicago in the fifties and sixties. Kids, cops, rich ladies, and one tall woman who kept showing up in shop windows and shadows.
The photographer was Vivian Maier. <short pause> She was a nanny.
For forty years she raised other people's kids, and walked the city with a camera around her neck.
She showed almost nobody. Her room was stacked to the ceiling with newspapers, so heavy the floor sagged.
When Maloof finally searched her name, he found her death notice. She had died a few days earlier.
He posted some photos online. They went viral. Museums called. A documentary about her got nominated for an Oscar.
Over a hundred thousand negatives, and almost nobody saw them while she was alive.
Her best shots spent decades in a box, buried under everything else.
Kind of like yours. Somewhere in your camera roll is a great photo, <chuckle> under four thousand screenshots.

```

## Shot ideas (rough, for the build)
- "In two thousand seven...": Low dolly down a cluttered auction hall (reuse the Smithsonian warehouse aisle, warmer light), a gavel comes down, a man lifts a dusty cardboard box. White flash on "great photo finds".
- "The box came from a storage locker...": Blurred whip to a row of storage units; one roll-up door rattles open on boxes stacked to the top.
- "Inside were negatives...": Close crane over the open box: film strips spill out, a canister rolls across the floor.
- "John Maloof was writing...": He holds a strip up to a desk lamp, squinting, expecting buildings.
- "Instead he found Chicago...": The strip's frame grows into a black-and-white cartoon street (the one place the palette goes grey), camera trucking past kids, a cop, a lady with a hat, and a tall woman's reflection in a shop window.
- "The photographer was Vivian Maier...": The reflection steps out: Vivian, wide hat, Rolleiflex at her chest, pushing a pram.
- "For forty years...": Tracking shot along a sidewalk, Vivian walking with a kid, raising the camera, looking down into it.
- "She showed almost nobody...": Crane up inside her small room: newspaper towers to the ceiling, the floor visibly bowing, one stack wobbling.
- "When Maloof finally searched...": His laptop glow in a dark room, a small newspaper notice; he leans back.
- "He posted some photos..." : Photos fly out of the laptop onto a wall of frames, a gallery fills with visitors, a small gold statue glint (the one "spark" moment).
- "Over a hundred thousand negatives..." and "Her best shots...": Pull back from the gallery to the lone box on the floor, lit by one lamp.
- Callback line and plug: The box's film strips lay flat into a phone photo grid of screenshots, one real-looking good photo glowing near the bottom. The real picker/results screens on a phone, SimpleShrink icon clear of it, end.
